Abstract

Action-conditioned latent world models predict future visual representations, enabling zero-shot goal-conditioned robot planning and control. However, their predictions for fine-grained spatial and rotational actions are unreliable for full 7-DoF end-effector control. To address this gap, we introduce DUET-DINO, a simultaneous cross-view latent world model that jointly learns action-conditioned predictions from static side- and wrist-camera observations through cross-view conditioning. By exploiting complementary global scene and gripper-centric information, DUET-DINO enables latent planning over the full 7-DoF action space. Across spatially-diverse reach, orientation-intensive angled-reach, and multi-goal grasp-and-lift tasks, DUET-DINO consistently outperforms single-view and independent dual-view baselines, achieving 92% success on reach, 72.5% on angled-reach, and 60.0% on lift tasks. DUET-DINO is trained from scratch on DROID and RoboArena datasets and generalizes robustly under visual distribution shifts. We further show that while V-JEPA 2 wrist-view predictions underestimate visual dynamics induced by fine-grained actions, DINOv3 predictions better capture action-conditioned scene changes, leading to stronger downstream planning.

Simultaneous Cross-View World Modeling

Cross-view conditioning connects the two perspectives before predicting what happens next.

Visual encoding

A frozen DINOv3 encoder maps synchronized side and wrist observations into patch-level latent features.

Cross-view conditioning

Cross-attention lets each stream query the other. View-specific predictors use this context, the action, and the robot state to predict future latents.

Goal-conditioned planning

Cross-entropy method (CEM) planning optimizes 7-DoF action sequences against a normalized dual-view goal cost, then replans after execution.

Zero-shot Latent Planning

Side- and wrist-view goal images specify the task. The model rolls out candidate 7-DoF action sequences and evaluates their predicted distance to the goal.

NORMALIZED DUAL-VIEW GOAL COST

Ji = (ℓiside + ℓiwrist) / (ℓside + ℓwrist)0

Each ℓ is an L1 distance to the corresponding goal latent. The denominator is the cost of a zero-action rollout over the same horizon.

A weighted top-k CEM update favors lower-cost action sequences. Simulation uses 800 candidates, 50 selected candidates, and two CEM iterations. Cross-attention and predictor heads are trained on 62,877 DROID and 5,856 RoboArena trajectories.

Angled Reach

Four tasks require coordinated translation and clockwise or counterclockwise yaw. Success requires position error ≤ 5 cm and angular error ≤ 12° within 100 steps.

DUET-DINO achieves 72.5% success, with mean final position error of 1.9 cm and mean final angular error of 22.7°.

Across 40 runs, DUET-DINO improves over independent dual-view DINOv3 (55%) and wrist-only DINOv3 (62.5%). DUET-VJEPA reaches 25%, matching its single-view and independent baselines. Final errors are measured at the last planning step for every run, so they need not satisfy the success thresholds.

Representation Analysis

Two complementary analyses examine how much predicted latents change and where their patch correspondences appear.

Latent Dynamics

Frozen DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2 encoders show changes of similar magnitude. Their action-conditioned predictors behave differently: DINOv3 follows the temporal pattern while generally overestimating its magnitude; V-JEPA 2 wrist predictions underestimate the changes.

Patch Correspondences

A reference patch on the target object is compared with patches at t, t+1, and the predicted t+1. Lower L1 distances mean higher similarity. DINOv3 preserves spatially coherent object correspondences, whereas V-JEPA 2 predictions also place similar features in unrelated regions.

Ablations

Cross-view attention

Removing cross-attention lowers reach success from 92% to 81%, and angled-reach success from 72.5% to 42.5%.

Camera pose shifts

Across 30 perturbed-camera reach runs, DUET-DINO and independent dual-view DINOv3 both reach 66.7%. Both side-only predictors reach 0%.

Training data

DROID-only training for 94.5k steps yields 78% reach success and 72.5% angled-reach success. The DROID + RoboArena model trains for 120k steps and reaches 92% and 72.5%, respectively.

Camera perturbations: ±20 cm in x/y, ±10 cm in z, and ±11.5° in roll/pitch/yaw. DROID-only and combined-data runs differ in both training data and training steps.
